Overview

Keeping a spacecraft pointed where it needs to be.

This project delivered the attitude determination and control system (ADCS) for a 3U CubeSat mission, covering everything from the control law on paper to the flight software running on the bus's microcontroller.

The spacecraft needed to reliably point a payload sensor at fixed ground targets and hold that pointing through orbital disturbances, without exceeding the power and mass budget typical of a small satellite bus.

Problem

Small satellites are mass- and power-constrained, which limits the actuators and sensors available for attitude control. The mission required sub-degree pointing accuracy using a reaction-wheel array sized to fit inside a 3U envelope, with detumble and safe-mode logic that could recover from any initial tip-off rate after deployment.

Approach

I derived the spacecraft's rotational dynamics and built a quaternion-based control law in Simulink, then validated it against a full 6-DOF orbital simulation including gravity-gradient and residual magnetic disturbance torques.

  • Designed a four-wheel pyramid reaction-wheel configuration for full 3-axis control with built-in redundancy
  • Implemented a quaternion feedback controller with a nonlinear detumble mode for post-deployment recovery
  • Fused magnetometer, sun sensor, and gyro data through an extended Kalman filter for attitude determination
  • Ported the control algorithm from Simulink to embedded C and integrated it with the flight software stack
  • Ran hardware-in-the-loop testing on an air-bearing table to validate the controller against real wheel dynamics
The biggest gap between simulation and hardware was wheel friction and stiction near zero speed — closing that gap took as much of the schedule as the original control law derivation.

Results

The system met its pointing requirement in both hardware-in-the-loop testing and post-launch commissioning, with the controller settling within tolerance well inside the allotted slew time.

What I'd do differently

Given more schedule margin, I'd characterize wheel friction on the bench earlier rather than folding it in after HIL testing surfaced it — that single change would have compressed the integration timeline noticeably.
